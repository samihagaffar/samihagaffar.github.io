CASE STUDY / 01

Dine on Demand

Bringing restaurant discovery, ordering, reservations, spending management, and delivery tracking into one iOS experience.

RoleProject Lead
Team3 members
TimelineJan 2022 – May 2022
PlatformiOS
Scope5 Long Island restaurants
ToolsSwift · Xcode · Firebase · Sketch
01 — THE PROBLEM

Dining often means switching between multiple apps.

Dining out can require separate applications for restaurant reservations and food ordering. We also identified food spending as an area where users could benefit from a more centralized experience.

The opportunity: bring these experiences together while helping users better understand their food spending.

03 — MY ROLE & CONTRIBUTION

Connecting product decisions to technical execution.

As project lead, I helped define product requirements, organized team work, created user stories, designed wireframes and mockups, prioritized features, coordinated sprint planning, and presented project demos.

I also worked closely with the technical implementation and helped keep the final product aligned with the defined requirements.

04 — DESIGN & USER FLOWS

Designing around real user workflows.

The team used Sketch to create interface designs before implementing them in Xcode.

Reservation flow and gift card purchase/redemption screens.

Weekly budget and spending history.

05 — PRODUCT DECISION
SECURITY & ACCESS

We changed the account approval workflow after identifying a security issue.

We changed the workflow so non-customer accounts required approval: employee accounts required restaurant manager approval, while manager accounts required administrator approval.

06 — SYSTEM & TECHNICAL IMPLEMENTATION

Six roles. One connected system.

The application supported Customer, Employee, Cook, Driver, Manager, and Admin roles, each with different access and responsibilities.

The product was built as a native iOS application using Swift and Xcode, with Firebase for database functionality and Sketch for UI design.

Use case diagram mapping roles to core system functionality.

Customer workflow and data flow.

07 — DEVELOPMENT PROCESS

Waterfall structure with Scrum practices.

08 — TESTING

Testing across individual features and connected workflows.

The team used black-box and white-box testing, including functional, smoke, and unit testing. We tested features, screens, customer flows, role-based functionality, GPS behavior, and spending updates.

Cross-role delivery tracking connecting driver and customer experiences.
