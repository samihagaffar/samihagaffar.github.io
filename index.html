CASE STUDY / 01

Dine on Demand

Bringing restaurant discovery, ordering, reservations, spending management, and delivery tracking into one iOS experience.

RoleProject Lead
Team3 members
TimelineJan 2022 – May 2022
PlatformiOS
Scope5 Long Island restaurants
ToolsSwift · Xcode · Firebase · Sketch
01 — OVERVIEW

One platform for the major parts of the dining experience.

Dine on Demand was an iOS application designed to bring several restaurant-related activities into one platform. Users could order food for pickup or delivery, make restaurant reservations, track their food spending through a weekly budget, and purchase and redeem gift cards.

The project was scoped around five restaurants on Long Island and was designed for users with iOS devices. Our three-person team used the Waterfall model to structure major phases while incorporating Scrum practices such as weekly meetings and sprint-based planning.

MY CONTRIBUTION

As project lead, I helped define product requirements, organize the team's work, create user stories, design wireframes and mockups, prioritize features, coordinate sprint planning, and present project demos. I also worked closely with the technical implementation and helped ensure the final product aligned with the defined requirements.

02 — THE PROBLEM

Dining often means switching between multiple applications.

Dining out can require separate applications for restaurant reservations and food ordering. We also identified food spending as an area where users could benefit from a more centralized experience.

Our team wanted to explore whether these experiences could be brought together into one application while helping users better understand how much they spend on food.

03 — COMPETITIVE RESEARCH

Existing products solved different pieces of the experience.

Rather than conducting primary user interviews or surveys, our team researched existing products and comparable applications to understand the features already available in the market.

OpenTable

Focused on restaurant reservations. Ordering was handled through the restaurant's website and there was no food-budgeting feature.

DoorDash

Supported restaurant browsing, ordering, and delivery, but did not provide restaurant reservations or budgeting.

Grubhub

Provided food ordering and delivery without combining restaurant reservations or food-budget tracking.

Uber Eats

Focused on food ordering and delivery without combining restaurant reservations and budgeting.

Product opportunity
Combine restaurant reservations, food ordering, and spending management within a single application.
04 — REQUIREMENTS & FEATURE DEFINITION

Turning the opportunity into defined product functionality.

01

Food Ordering & Delivery Tracking

Order food for pickup or delivery, view order status, and track the driver's location on a map.

02

Restaurant Reservations

Discover nearby restaurants, review information, and make reservations based on available dates and times.

03

Weekly Food Budget

Set a weekly spending limit and incorporate order spending into budget tracking.

04

Gift Cards

Purchase and redeem restaurant gift cards through the application.

Product scope: Five Long Island restaurants, iOS devices, and six account types with different levels of access and functionality.

05 — PRODUCT DECISIONS & ITERATION

Requirements evolved when implementation revealed new needs.

SECURITY DECISION

Account approval workflow

We identified a security issue with allowing users to create employee accounts without sufficient authorization. We changed the account creation process so non-customer accounts required approval.

Employee accounts required restaurant manager approval, while manager accounts required administrator approval.

FEATURE ITERATION

Expanded budget functionality

The original budget concept focused on a weekly spending limit. During development, we expanded it so users could review spending history and identify whether they had exceeded their budget during previous weeks.

06 — USER ROLES & ACCESS

Six roles with different responsibilities and permissions.

Customer

Reservations, orders, weekly budget, account management, gift cards, and order tracking.

Employee

View, modify, or cancel restaurant reservations and manage current orders.

Cook

View pending orders and update order status when food is ready.

Driver

View delivery orders, access delivery information, share location, and update delivery status.

Manager

Manage reservations and orders, view restaurant financial information, and approve employee requests.

Admin

Manage restaurant information, view analytics, manage accounts, and approve manager accounts.

ACCESS MODEL

Each role was mapped to specific actions and data access. Customers handled dining activities; restaurant staff managed reservations and orders; cooks updated preparation status; drivers handled delivery and location updates; managers approved employee accounts and managed restaurant operations; administrators managed restaurant-level information, analytics, and manager approvals.

07 — PRODUCT DESIGN & WIREFRAMES

Designing around the defined requirements and workflows.

After defining the core requirements and user roles, our team moved into the design phase. We used Sketch to create the application's interface designs before implementing them in Xcode.

The designs established the structure and flow of the application, including account access, restaurant discovery, reservations, ordering, budgeting, gift cards, and operational workflows.

FLOW 01

Restaurant Discovery & Reservations

Users could discover restaurants, review restaurant details, select available dates and times, and make or manage reservations.

FLOW 02

Food Ordering

Customers could choose pickup or delivery, place orders, review order history, and track delivery status.

FLOW 03

Weekly Budget

Customers could set a weekly food-spending limit and review previous spending to understand when they exceeded their budget.

FLOW 04

Gift Cards

Customers could purchase gift cards and redeem existing balances within the application.

08 — DEVELOPMENT & TECHNICAL IMPLEMENTATION

Native iOS implementation with role-based access.

The application was developed as a native iOS application using Xcode and Swift, with Firebase used for database functionality. UIKit was used as part of the front-end development, and Sketch was used for UI design.

The system supported six account types with different functionality based on each user's responsibilities. Security was incorporated through role-based access and encryption of sensitive information such as passwords and customer payment information.

FRONT END

iOS Application

Built with Xcode, Swift, and UIKit. The interface supported customer-facing experiences as well as operational workflows for employees, cooks, drivers, managers, and administrators.

DATA & SECURITY

Firebase + Role-Based Access

Firebase supported application data including accounts, restaurants, menus, reservations, and orders. Role-based access controlled functionality, while sensitive information such as passwords and payment information was encrypted.

CUSTOMER DATA FLOW

The customer workflow moved from authentication into core actions such as restaurant discovery, reservations, ordering, budgeting, gift cards, and order tracking. The same underlying data supported the connected experiences across the different account roles.

09 — PRODUCT DEVELOPMENT PROCESS

Waterfall structure with Scrum practices.

The Waterfall approach provided structure for the major phases, while Scrum practices helped organize work into sprints and maintain regular communication.

Sprint 1

Planning, competitive research, product approach, system diagrams, and data modeling.

Sprint 2

Sign-in, sign-up, welcome page, and initial weekly budget functionality.

Sprint 3

Account details, gift cards, restaurant discovery, and restaurant information.

Sprint 4

Reservation history, order history, reservations, ordering, pickup/delivery, and budget completion.

Sprint 5

Employee and manager functionality, reservations, orders, approvals, and financial information.

Sprint 6

Driver and cook functionality, delivery tracking, and order preparation workflows.

Sprint 7

Administrator functionality, UI improvements, and encryption of sensitive database information.

Sprint 8

Final testing, technical documentation, presentation, and project completion.

TEAM COLLABORATION

As project lead, I helped coordinate work across the three-person team, communicate progress, organize upcoming work, and keep development aligned with the planned product.

10 — TESTING & QUALITY ASSURANCE

Testing individual features and connected workflows.

Testing was performed throughout development using both black-box and white-box methods.

  • Black-box testing: test data and multiple test cases verified expected results.
  • Functional testing: features, buttons, functions, and screens were tested against intended behavior.
  • Smoke testing: major functions were tested together, including connected delivery workflows.
  • White-box testing: code-level testing and debugging verified code paths.
  • Unit testing: individual methods and functions were tested, including GPS tracking, delivery addresses, and spending updates.
BLACK-BOX

Functional Testing

Test cases verified that features, buttons, screens, ordering, reservations, gift cards, account information, and role-specific functionality produced the expected results.

INTEGRATION

Smoke Testing

Major functions were tested together. Delivery tracking was one example where the driver's location and the customer's view needed to update as part of the same workflow.

WHITE-BOX

Code-Level Testing

Code paths were traced and debugged to verify that the application's logic executed correctly.

UNIT TESTING

Individual Functions

Individual methods were tested, including functionality related to GPS tracking, delivery addresses, and updating customer spending data.

CASE STUDY / 02

Auto Insurance App

An auto insurance platform designed to provide affordable, discounted coverage while simplifying the journey from insurance request to quote, policy, and payment.

Project LeadAffordable CoverageRAD + ScrumSwiftFirebase
RoleProject Lead
Team3 members
TimelineOct 2021 – Dec 2021
PlatformiOS
MethodsRAD · Scrum
ToolsSwift · Xcode · Firebase
01 — THE PROBLEM

Designing an insurance experience around affordable coverage and a clear quote-to-policy journey.

The team reviewed existing insurance applications including Allstate, Metromile, State Farm, and Progressive. These products offered broad functionality such as claims, roadside assistance, payments, agents, and coverage management.

The opportunity: focus the experience around the core journey of obtaining and purchasing auto insurance.

02 — PROPOSED EXPERIENCE
01

Account

Create an account or sign in.

02

Policy Form

Enter the information needed for a quote.

03

Quote

Receive and review the insurance offer.

04

Payment

Accept the offer and submit payment.

05

Documents

Receive the policy and coverage statement.

03 — MY ROLE & CONTRIBUTION

Leading the team, documentation, and system definition.

As project lead, I led a three-person team, coordinated project progress, supported requirements analysis, and helped keep the team aligned with the project timeline.

My contributions included project documentation, system diagrams, wireframes, and functional requirements, along with communicating product decisions throughout development.

04 — COMPETITIVE RESEARCH

The team compared the proposed experience with Allstate, Metromile, State Farm, and Progressive. Existing products offered many useful insurance services, but the proposed app intentionally narrowed the experience to the quote and policy journey.

Allstate

Quotes, safe-driving rewards, roadside assistance, repair shops, claims, payments, and agents.

Metromile

Pay-per-mile insurance plus quotes, payments, claims, alerts, and roadside assistance.

State Farm

Quotes, safe-driving rewards, agents, claims, payments, and coverage information.

Progressive

Quotes, rewards, claims, payments, coverage, and bundled insurance options.

05 — SYSTEM ANALYSIS

Mapping the quote and policy workflow before implementation.

The report documented the system context, customer insurance information flow, customer acceptance flow, database schema, and entity relationships.

06 — PRODUCT INTERFACE

Turning the requirements into a focused mobile flow.

The implemented screens covered sign-in, dashboard, card replacement, customer information, premium statements, vehicle information, registration, and policy documents.

07 — PRODUCT DECISION
DATABASE SCOPE

The team changed the original two-database plan because of project cost constraints.

The original plan called for separate insurance-company and DMV databases. Because creating a second Firebase database would have introduced additional expense, the team combined the information into one database.

08 — DEVELOPMENT PROCESS

RAD + Scrum for a short delivery window.

The team used Rapid Application Development alongside Agile Scrum because the project had a short completion window and required adaptation as development progressed. Work was organized into sprints with weekly meetings to track progress.

09 — TESTING & LIMITATIONS

Testing focused on core functions and data behavior.

The team tested functions, features, buttons, screens, database uploads/downloads, PDF generation, integration behavior, and major application functionality through functional, unit, integration, and smoke testing.

Key limitations included time, the learning curve for Firebase/Xcode and PDF generation, and the cost constraint that led to combining the databases.

10 — OUTCOME & LEARNINGS

A complete prototype centered on the core insurance journey.

Leadership

I managed team progress, maintained the timeline, and coordinated project documentation and diagrams.

Product focus

Competitive research helped us narrow the experience around the highest-priority insurance journey.

Technical understanding

The project strengthened my experience with Xcode, Swift, Firebase, system diagrams, and mobile workflows.

Constraints

Project decisions had to balance user experience, technical feasibility, time, and cost.

CASE STUDY / 03

Connect NYC

A cross-platform networking and resource application designed to help university and organization members connect, discover events, access food delivery, and manage their profiles.

Product Team MemberGroups & MessagingEventsFlutterFirebase
RoleProduct Team Member
Team5 members
TimelineSep 2020 – Dec 2020
PlatformCross-platform
MethodsIncremental · Scrum
ToolsFlutter · Dart · Firebase · Jira
01 — THE PROBLEM

Remote learning made it harder for students and organization members to build connections.

The project was created during the COVID-19 pandemic, when remote work and limited in-person interaction made it harder for people within universities, businesses, and social groups to connect and access shared resources.

The opportunity: create an organization-specific place for communication, events, resources, and community discovery.

02 — COMPETITIVE RESEARCH

The team reviewed Discord, LinkedIn, and Slack. These products provided useful communication and networking capabilities, but the proposed experience was designed around a specific organization and its members rather than a general-purpose network.

03 — PROPOSED EXPERIENCE
01

Groups & Messaging

Help members discover and communicate with groups and contacts.

02

Events

Post organization events and keep track of them.

03

Food Delivery

Give students convenient access to nearby restaurants.

04

Profiles

Manage user information and identity within the organization.

04 — MY ROLE & CONTRIBUTION

Contributing product structure and technical documentation.

I contributed to roadmap planning and translated business needs into epics and user stories. I also created technical documentation, including workflows, database schemas, and user manuals, and participated in Agile planning sessions to refine requirements and support sprint execution.

The project also strengthened my ability to collaborate with teammates who had different work styles and backgrounds.

05 — PRODUCT ITERATION
USER EXPERIENCE

The team added navigation and separated payment into its own flow to make the app easier to use.

The original design did not include a bottom navigation bar. It was added so users could move between features without repeatedly returning to the main menu. The food-ordering payment experience was also moved to a dedicated screen, and email verification was added as an additional security measure.

06 — SYSTEM DESIGN

From organization needs to technical architecture.

The report documented context, use-case, data-flow, database, and entity-relationship diagrams before implementation.

07 — PRODUCT INTERFACE

Designing the experience across multiple community features.

The interface evolved from early concepts into a working cross-platform experience spanning profiles, groups, events, food delivery, and messaging.

08 — DEVELOPMENT PROCESS

Incremental development with Scrum practices and Jira.

The team used an incremental software development process to allow changes throughout the development cycle. Weekly JAD sessions were used to discuss tasks and progress, while Jira was used to organize issues and break work into sprints.

09 — TOOLS & IMPLEMENTATION
Flutter

Cross-platform UI and application framework using Dart.

Firebase

User authentication and real-time database functionality.

Jira

Issues, stories, tasks, and sprint organization.

Proto.io

Initial UI/UX design and prototyping.

10 — TESTING & OUTCOME

Testing helped the team integrate independently built features into one application.

Initial testing uncovered merge conflicts when the team combined branches after working separately. The team resolved the conflicts and tested the merged application across several devices. Most tests succeeded.

The report identified future opportunities including expanding the app beyond NYIT to other universities and companies and adding organization-specific customization.

Key learning

The project strengthened my collaboration skills, taught me to adapt to different work styles, and showed me the importance of consistent documentation and detailed system diagrams.
